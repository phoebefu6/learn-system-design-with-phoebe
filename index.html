Learn System Design with Phoebe

Eight sessions, 45 minutes each, and one platform you architect across all of them. Every other course on this shelf teaches you to build a thing. This one teaches you to decide which thing, on evidence, under a budget, in front of people who will have to live with the answer. There is no best architecture in here. There is a requirement, four meters that fight each other, and a decision you have to sign your name to.

The eight sessions 🎛️ · one platform, architected across all of them

The running project is Daybreak, the coffee-subscription brand you already met in the SQL and warehouse courses. It is three years old, it has one Postgres, and its dashboards query production. You are the person who has to decide what it becomes next, and write down why.

📐 Session 1 · easy

Requirements to constraints

Nobody hands you an architecture problem. They hand you a complaint. Turning "the dashboard is slow" into four numbers you can design against, on paper, before any technology is named.

▶ Start here 🧱 Session 2 · easy

Boundaries and contracts

Where one team's problem stops being another team's problem. The three couplings that actually hurt, what belongs in a data contract, and why the producer's build should go red instead of your dashboard.

who owns what 🗄️ Session 3 · medium

Storage and serving

Row store, column store, lake, stream - chosen by access pattern, not by fashion. The freshness-cost curve, PACELC, and why designing to the average latency is designing for nobody.

the curve 🕸️ Session 4 · medium

Orchestration topology

Cron knows what time it is. An orchestrator knows what finished. Idempotency as a design property, backfills that are boring, and SLAs on data rather than on services.

what finished 🎛️ Session 5 · hands-on

Designing for failure

The tradeoff board. Nine levers, four meters, two requirements - and the moment where switching the requirement without touching a lever flips the verdict. Plus: retries do not fix a contract break.

★ the simulator 🛡️ Session 6 · hands-on

The governed shell

Access, lineage, PII boundaries, retention, deletion and cost attribution - designed at the boundary rather than bolted on afterwards, because deletion cannot be retrofitted into a lake.

every copy is an obligation 🤖 Session 7 · hardest

AI as a system component

A model is a latency budget, a cost line and a failure mode. Retrieval indexes go stale, agent loops have unbounded cost, and DORA 2025 found AI raises throughput and instability at the same time.

the small box 📝 Session 8 · hardest

The design review

ADRs, one-way and two-way doors, and the four questions a good reviewer asks. Capstone: take apart a deliberately broken Daybreak architecture and write the decision record that fixes it.
